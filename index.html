BIOGRAPHY

Community leadership and responsibility.

Martin Wek Tong is a community leader and business executive aspiring to represent Constituency 8, Gogrial East County, in the National Parliament of South Sudan.

He serves as Chairman of the Biong-Makiech Community Association in Juba. His public profile includes community association leadership and engagement around shared community concerns.

Martin is also the Chief Executive Officer of Panloi Company, which works in petroleum waste management, and the Chief Executive Officer of Kan Capital Company.

His proposed parliamentary platform focuses on listening to residents, raising local priorities through responsible parliamentary work, and being accountable about progress and challenges. It identifies education, healthcare, agriculture and livelihoods, essential infrastructure, economic opportunity, peace and security, and the participation of women and young people as key areas for consultation and advocacy.

MESSAGE FROM THE CANDIDATE

Representation begins
with listening.

My fellow citizens of Constituency 8, I seek your trust because I believe our communities deserve responsible representation, a strong voice and leadership that listens. Our people have concerns about education, healthcare, roads, livelihoods, peace and opportunities for young people. These concerns deserve serious attention and honest discussion.

My proposed priorities come from the need to improve everyday life in our communities. I want to advocate for better learning conditions, accessible healthcare, stronger agricultural livelihoods, essential infrastructure and meaningful participation by women and young people. These priorities must be shaped by listening to residents and understanding the needs of different communities.

I understand that a parliamentary seat is not a promise to solve every problem alone. The responsibility is to represent people, contribute to lawmaking, scrutinise public decisions and budgets, raise community concerns and work with the institutions responsible for delivering services. Progress requires cooperation, realistic planning and the responsible use of public resources.

I invite our elders, women, youth, community leaders and all residents to share their views and hold me accountable. I will value honest feedback, respectful dialogue and unity. Together, we can work towards a more peaceful, inclusive and prosperous future for Gogrial East.

Martin Wek Tong
Aspiring Candidate · National Parliamentary Seat

SEVEN PROPOSED PILLARS

Priorities for representation.

Issues proposed for consultation, parliamentary work and engagement with responsible institutions.

01

Education

Advocate for functional learning environments, essential learning materials, girls’ participation and constructive engagement with education authorities.

  • School facilities
  • Learning resources
  • Girls’ education
02

Healthcare

Raise community concerns about functional health facilities, maternal and child care, preventive services, essential medicines and the needs of health workers.

  • Facility conditions
  • Maternal and child care
  • Medicines and staffing
03

Agriculture and livelihoods

Advocate for locally relevant agricultural advice, appropriate inputs, storage, market connections, practical skills and responsible rural enterprise.

  • Extension services
  • Storage and markets
  • Livelihood skills
04

Roads and essential infrastructure

Raise the need for transport links, safe water, transparent public project priorities and maintenance planning.

  • Transport access
  • Safe water
  • Transparent priorities
05

Economic opportunity

Encourage partnerships that help people access practical skills, enterprise development and wider opportunities in local economic life.

  • Skills
  • Enterprise
  • Partnerships
06

Peace and security

Promote respectful dialogue, community cooperation, early engagement and peaceful approaches to resolving disputes.

  • Dialogue
  • Community trust
  • Peaceful dispute resolution
07

Youth and women

Advocate for meaningful participation by women and young people in public dialogue and access to skills and livelihood opportunities.

  • Participation
  • Skills and livelihoods
  • Inclusive consultation

HOW THE WORK SHOULD PROCEED

Accountability is part of representation.

PHASE 01

Listen and identify priorities

Consult residents and community representatives, record concerns and clarify which issues need attention first.

PHASE 02

Advocate and follow up

Raise priorities through legislative work, parliamentary oversight, budget scrutiny and engagement with responsible institutions.

PHASE 03

Report and review

Communicate progress, obstacles and next steps, then invite feedback and review priorities with the people represented.

This is a proposed advocacy platform, not a list of completed achievements or a guarantee of specific outcomes. Delivery depends on the parliamentary mandate, available budgets, applicable public processes and the institutions responsible for implementation.
