MESSAGE FROM THE CANDIDATE

Representation begins
with listening.

My fellow citizens of Constituency 8, I seek your trust because I believe our communities deserve responsible representation, a strong voice and leadership that listens. Our people have concerns about education, healthcare, roads, livelihoods, peace and opportunities for young people. These concerns deserve serious attention and honest discussion.

This manifesto sets out 18 objectives for Constituency 8, from peace and security to public accountability. Every payam deserves to be heard and served. The proposals are intended to guide consultation, parliamentary advocacy, oversight and public review. I want to advocate for better learning conditions, accessible healthcare, stronger agricultural livelihoods, essential infrastructure and meaningful participation by women and young people. The named payams are Pathuon East, Pathuon West, Toch East, Toch West, Toch North and Nyang. The priorities should be refined through consultation and evidence about the needs of each community.

I understand that a parliamentary seat is not a promise to solve every problem alone. The responsibility is to represent people, contribute to lawmaking, scrutinise public decisions and budgets, raise community concerns and work with the institutions responsible for delivering services. Progress requires cooperation, realistic planning and the responsible use of public resources.

I invite our elders, women, youth, community leaders and all residents to share their views and hold me accountable. I will value honest feedback, respectful dialogue and unity. Together, we can work towards a more peaceful, inclusive and prosperous future for Gogrial East.

Martin Wek Tong
Aspiring Candidate · National Parliamentary Seat

THE FULL MANIFESTO

18 objectives for Constituency 8.

Expanded from the supplied manifesto document. Each objective includes proposed actions, an implementation approach and an intended outcome.

01

Peace and security

Proposed priorities

Prevent revenge attacks and community violence; resolve cattle, land and water disputes peacefully; protect civilians through lawful security measures; involve chiefs, women and youth in dialogue.

Implementation approach

Map urgent risks and trusted local contacts; convene dialogue and record agreed solutions; refer threats and track unresolved incidents.

Intended outcome: Safer travel, farming and community life.

02

Essential healthcare

Proposed priorities

Staff existing clinics and keep medicines available; strengthen malaria treatment and immunisation; reach remote settlements through scheduled outreach; improve emergency referral and trauma support.

Implementation approach

Audit staffing, supplies and referral gaps; pursue funded repairs, medicines and outreach; check service days, stock-outs and referrals.

Intended outcome: Working clinics and more reliable treatment.

03

Mothers and children

Proposed priorities

Expand skilled maternity and newborn care; improve access to emergency birth referral; screen children for malnutrition and missed vaccines; support mothers with practical nutrition advice.

Implementation approach

Locate communities facing the longest care delays; seek midwife coverage and referral transport; follow vaccination and nutrition treatment coverage.

Intended outcome: Safer births and healthier children.

04

Education

Proposed priorities

Keep teachers present and classrooms usable; provide books, desks and learning materials; help girls and interrupted learners stay in school; expand inclusive secondary and vocational pathways.

Implementation approach

Assess school needs with teachers and parents; seek teacher support, materials and repairs; review attendance, retention and learning.

Intended outcome: Children attending school and learning.

05

Water and sanitation

Proposed priorities

Repair broken water points; extend safe water to underserved settlements; improve sanitation at schools and clinics; keep maintenance affordable and accountable.

Implementation approach

Map water points, shortages and water quality; seek repairs, trained mechanics and spare parts; check functionality, access and repair times.

Intended outcome: Reliable safe water with fewer breakdowns.

06

Agriculture

Proposed priorities

Deliver suitable seeds and tools before planting; expand practical extension and farmer groups; reduce losses through drying and storage; link producers with reliable buyers.

Implementation approach

Agree seasonal needs and fair input selection; seek timely inputs and field demonstrations; track yields, harvest losses and sales.

Intended outcome: Stronger harvests and household food security.

07

Livestock

Proposed priorities

Expand vaccination and veterinary access; improve disease alerts and local animal care; agree peaceful grazing and water access; support fair markets and safe milk handling.

Implementation approach

Review veterinary gaps and seasonal herd movement; pursue vaccination and animal-health support; follow disease reports and access disputes.

Intended outcome: Healthier herds and protected livelihoods.

08

Local businesses

Proposed priorities

Provide bookkeeping and enterprise skills; support savings groups and responsible finance; challenge unauthorised fees and trading barriers; improve markets, storage and local processing.

Implementation approach

Identify traders’ finance, fee and market barriers; seek training, fair fees and viable investment; track business survival and market improvements.

Intended outcome: Local enterprises able to trade and grow.

09

Youth employment

Proposed priorities

Match skills training to real local demand; develop supervised apprenticeships; make opportunities fair and publicly accessible; link graduates to work and business mentoring.

Implementation approach

Map employers, skills demand and providers; seek funded training and practical placements; track graduates earning income after six months.

Intended outcome: Skills that lead to work and earnings.

10

Women and girls

Proposed priorities

Support women’s enterprises and training; keep girls learning and returning to school; give women a voice in public decisions; strengthen safe, confidential protection referrals.

Implementation approach

Consult women on access and safety barriers; seek education, livelihood and referral support; review participation, retention and income gains.

Intended outcome: Greater opportunity, safety and participation.

11

Roads and bridges

Proposed priorities

Open access to clinics, schools and markets; repair critical crossings and drainage; fund maintenance alongside construction; demand safe works and sound engineering.

Implementation approach

Rank blocked routes through technical assessment; seek costed repairs and budget allocations; inspect quality and follow maintenance needs.

Intended outcome: Essential routes remaining usable in the rains.

12

Floods and environment

Proposed priorities

Prepare warnings and safe evacuation routes; protect water sources and public facilities; promote resilient farming and suitable tree planting; seek fair relief and livelihood recovery.

Implementation approach

Identify hazards, safe sites and vulnerable residents; agree warnings and preparedness before rains; review damage, assistance gaps and recovery.

Intended outcome: Communities better prepared for seasonal shocks.

13

Energy and connectivity

Proposed priorities

Power essential health, water and learning services; support suitable solar systems with maintenance; pursue affordable rural mobile coverage; keep information accessible offline too.

Implementation approach

Assess power, coverage and operating costs; seek technical designs and funded service pilots; verify uptime, affordability and local access.

Intended outcome: Useful systems that remain operational.

14

Land and justice

Proposed priorities

Clarify lawful land and resource procedures; document agreements on shared access; promote impartial complaint handling; protect residents from intimidation and abuse.

Implementation approach

Identify recurring disputes and legal referral routes; seek legal awareness and clear records; follow referrals through competent institutions.

Intended outcome: Fair procedures and peaceful dispute resolution.

15

Inclusion and welfare

Proposed priorities

Improve access for persons with disabilities; include older people and vulnerable children; seek fair support for displaced and returning families; protect privacy and prevent beneficiary favouritism.

Implementation approach

Identify service barriers with affected groups; seek accessible repairs and appropriate referrals; review assistance coverage and exclusion complaints.

Intended outcome: Essential services reaching overlooked households.

16

Sport and culture

Proposed priorities

Create safe opportunities for sport and talent; strengthen cultural learning and community links; include girls and young people with disabilities; use existing spaces with affordable upkeep.

Implementation approach

Identify safe spaces and local organisers; seek equipment, coaching and clear safety rules; review participation and sustained use.

Intended outcome: Talent, belonging and constructive youth activity.

17

National representation

Proposed priorities

Carry constituency needs into Parliament; scrutinise budgets, releases and public delivery; advocate fair resources for underserved areas; engage on laws affecting daily life.

Implementation approach

Compile verified priorities across all six payams; submit issues and seek written institutional responses; report parliamentary action and unresolved gaps.

Intended outcome: A visible constituency voice in national decisions.

18

Public accountability

Proposed priorities

Keep accessible contact and complaints channels; consult every payam regularly; publish progress, funding status and delays; disclose conflicts of interest and prevent favouritism.

Implementation approach

Establish focal contacts and a consultation calendar; issue quarterly scorecards and follow up complaints; hold an annual public review of commitments.

Intended outcome: Residents able to question and assess leadership.

These are proposed commitments for advocacy, consultation, oversight and partnerships—not completed achievements or guaranteed results. Implementation depends on the parliamentary mandate, verified needs, approvals, budgets and the responsible public institutions and qualified service providers.

HOW THE WORK SHOULD PROCEED

Accountability is part of representation.

PHASE 01

Listen and identify priorities

Consult residents and community representatives, record concerns and clarify which issues need attention first.

PHASE 02

Advocate and follow up

Raise priorities through legislative work, parliamentary oversight, budget scrutiny and engagement with responsible institutions.

PHASE 03

Report and review

Communicate progress, obstacles and next steps, then invite feedback and review priorities with the people represented.

This is a proposed advocacy platform, not a list of completed achievements or a guarantee of specific outcomes. Delivery depends on the parliamentary mandate, available budgets, applicable public processes and the institutions responsible for implementation.
